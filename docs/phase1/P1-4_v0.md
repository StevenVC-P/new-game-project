# P1-4: E13 V0, Observing Local Value

The observer (`tests/sim/value_observer.gd`) and its report (`tests/sim/value_report.gd`) answer the eight questions in `docs/economic_foundations/E13_local_value.md`, section 4.

**Runs:** six arrangements, 5 lands × 8 starts, ordinary, no player, 6 years, on `0f3912a`. Play is identical with the observer on (only timing fields differ). The full report is `ngg-v0-logs/v0_report.log`.

**A bug, found and fixed.** The first runs read each family's hungry days on the month's last day, after the month's turn had reset them, so Q2 and Q3 reported no hunger at all. The divergence check now counts them day by day (`686d197`). Private and private-with-reserve are rerunning; Q2 and Q3 come from that rerun.

## Answers

| # | Question | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is a year's forecast believable? | **For food and wood, forecast from what the town actually made recently** (trailing three months). Food's error is about 0.12 of turnover at every horizon, in every arrangement. The proposal's "work families hold now" forecast (`get_expected_output`) does as well in pooled towns but up to twice as badly under private compacts (food 0.22–0.24, wood 0.4–0.54). **For tools, the opposite.** Their demand is lumpy, so trailing output misleads (error up to 1.0 under private), and expected output is better (0.25–0.5) |
| 2 | Does it warn in time? | Pending the rerun |
| 3 | What causes hunger? | Pending the rerun. From the S5d logs: a third of private hunger days fall while another family holds food beyond a month |
| 4 | Does the access signal behave? | Yes. Physical and accessible coverage are identical in pooled towns (gap 0 in 1,890 months). Under compacts the median gap is 0, but its 90th percentile is 0.23 (private), 0.39 (private with reserve) and 0.69 (shared with reserve), where food sits in a reserve families can't reach unless in danger |
| 5 | Does intermittent demand work? | Continuous claims dominate (about 0.9 million units a month across towns) against committed building (about 3,600) and periodic tool upkeep (about 25,000). With supply set aside, families would build about 8,000 homes of dressed stone or brick: latent demand the town never sees today, as the proposal warned |
| 6 | Which anchor works? | Market prices match the grain table almost exactly wherever exchange is thin (food 1.00, wood 1.5, tools 10). Under shared and private compacts, wood trades at 1.1–1.35 and tools at 5–9.35. So the table overvalues tools when markets work. The scarcity factor for food centres on 1 (0.86–1.02) |
| 7 | Is it stable and meaningful? | **Food: yes.** Monthly swing 0.08–0.09, at a limit 2–3% of months. **Wood: mostly** (swing 0.03–0.07, at a limit 20–30%). **Tools: no.** At a limit 45–71% of months, because periodic demand swings coverage between none and plenty |
| 8 | What does it cost? | The observer's own pass costs 7–16 ms a month under 10 households, 26–63 ms at 10–24, and 206–328 ms at 25+, mostly the latent-home choice for every family. V1 needs a value per town per month, not this observer, so its cost will be far lower |

**Sensitivity (food, all arrangements):** at target 1.25 and power 1, the value's median is 0.96 and it is at a limit 2% of months. A power of 2 pushes it to a limit 13–22% of months, and a power of 0.5 never.

## For V1 and V2

- **V1 can start with food and wood.** Use the trailing-output forecast, target 1.25 and power 1, and accessible coverage for the town reference (owner's decision 1).
- **Tools need their own treatment before V1 values them.** Smooth periodic claims over their interval, and forecast from expected output.
- **The grain table's tool value should yield to evidence** where markets trade (5–9 against 10).
- **V2's timing** depends on Q2 and Q3 from the rerun. The S5d evidence (a third of private hunger beside spare food) already argues that access failure shapes private results materially, which the proposal named as the reason to bring V2 before S10.
