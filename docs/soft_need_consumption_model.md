# Soft Need Consumption Model

This document defines the intended transition from visible soft-need pressure to actual soft-need consumption. It is a planning model only. It should prevent accidental coupling between demand display, prosperity, production, inventory, and future money systems before implementation begins.

## Purpose

The current goods-demand sprint has built demand visibility and status:

- food can be consumed through a need bucket
- household goods can appear as pressure
- family demand can be inspected on selected homes
- workplace instability can be warned about
- household prosperity can be displayed as an abstract status
- clothing can appear as display-only pressure

Soft-need consumption is the next boundary. It is where non-survival needs begin consuming goods and may eventually influence household prosperity. That makes it riskier than display-only pressure: a consumption rule can quietly become a penalty, balance lever, or hidden production requirement. This document records the intended model before `feature/soft-need-consumption-v0` is implemented.

## Definitions

- `hard needs`: survival or retention needs such as food and shelter. Shortage can reasonably affect population stability, growth, or production reliability.
- `soft needs`: quality-of-life needs such as household goods and clothing. Shortage should begin as comfort/prosperity pressure, not survival failure.
- `demand`: the amount of a need generated by families, homes, workplaces, or later civic buildings.
- `supply`: the current city stockpile amount of goods that can satisfy a need bucket.
- `satisfaction`: the portion of demand covered by available supply during a demand resolution pass.
- `shortage`: demand that could have been supplied by an existing goods category, but was not fully satisfied.
- `unavailable need`: demand for a bucket that has no current goods capable of satisfying it.
- `consumption`: removal of goods from the city stockpile to satisfy demand.
- `display-only pressure`: visible demand/status that does not consume goods or apply effects.
- `household-derived demand`: demand calculated from household/family records, such as population, housing state, or lifecycle state.
- `city-level aggregation`: the city sums demand from households, homes, workplaces, and later civic buildings, then resolves that aggregate against stockpiles.

## Proposed Soft-Need Categories

Start with these soft needs:

- `household_goods`: domestic goods that represent home comfort, storage, household utility, and family stability.
- `clothing`: personal/family need that can later support comfort, seasonal stability, and prosperity.

Future soft needs may include:

- home improvement goods
- tools as household upkeep goods
- fuel
- medicine
- luxury goods
- education access
- religious or civic access

Those future categories should stay out of v0 unless explicitly scoped.

## Recommended V0 Implementation Boundaries

`feature/soft-need-consumption-v0` should:

- consume only already-recognized soft-need goods
- keep consumption simple and deterministic
- keep demand city-visible and household-readable
- preserve existing display output
- track satisfaction ratios
- avoid harsh penalties
- prefer prosperity modifiers or status notes over survival penalties
- skip buckets whose supply goods do not exist yet

`feature/soft-need-consumption-v0` should not:

- add new production chains
- add wool, sheep, textile, or clothing production
- add household private inventory
- add market or trade routing
- add hidden penalties
- add winter or climate effects
- add per-family ownership simulation
- add money, savings, prices, credit, debt, or currency

## Consumption Model Proposal

The simple model:

1. Households generate demand.
2. The city aggregates soft-need demand.
3. City stockpiles satisfy demand by need bucket.
4. Goods mapped to a need bucket may be consumed toward that need.
5. Consumption is capped by available stockpile.
6. Satisfaction ratio is calculated as `consumed / demand`.
7. If demand is zero, status is `none`.
8. If no current goods can satisfy the bucket, status remains `unavailable`.
9. If goods exist for the bucket but stockpile is zero, status may be `unmet`.
10. If some demand is satisfied but not all, status may be `low`.
11. If all demand is satisfied, status is `met`.

Pseudo-flow:

```text
demand = aggregate_household_soft_need(bucket)
available_goods = current goods mapped to bucket

if demand <= 0:
    status = none
elif available_goods is empty:
    status = unavailable
else:
    consumed = consume_bucket(bucket, demand)
    satisfaction_ratio = consumed / demand
    status = met, low, or unmet
```

Consumption should be deterministic. If multiple goods satisfy a bucket, consume them in helper-defined order unless a later quality/preference model changes that.

## Household Vs City Responsibility

Households generate demand. The city currently aggregates and resolves that demand.

This is acceptable for v0 because:

- current resources live in `city.resources`
- current pressure UI is city-level
- household inventory does not exist
- city-level aggregation is easy to inspect and test

Future household/home inventory may move satisfaction closer to the household:

- home food reserves
- clothing owned by household
- household goods stored in the home
- emergency buffers during city shortages

V0 soft-need consumption should not require household inventories.

## Prosperity Interaction

Current household prosperity is display-only. It is an abstract status, not money or exact wealth.

Soft needs should eventually modify prosperity, but the first consumption branch should be cautious. Two possible approaches:

- `A`: report satisfaction ratios only, without modifying prosperity
- `B`: apply a small transparent prosperity modifier

Recommended v0: choose `A`.

Reason: the current UI just made family demand, soft pressure, workplace instability, and household prosperity legible. Adding consumption and prosperity effects in the same first branch risks making it unclear whether a status changed because of food, shelter, household goods, clothing, or a hidden rule.

Soft needs should not create survival penalties in v0. Missing household goods or clothing should not remove population, block assignment, stop production, or force lifecycle changes.

## Clothing-Specific Note

Clothing pressure already exists, but clothing supply does not.

Current state:

- `BUCKET_CLOTHING = "clothing"` exists.
- Clothing demand is family-derived by household population.
- No current goods satisfy the clothing bucket.
- No `wool` or `clothing` stockpile keys exist.
- No textile production exists.
- Clothing does not affect prosperity, penalties, consumption, survival, or production.

Do not make clothing consumption active until a clothing good exists.

Consider a separate branch before soft consumption:

- `feature/clothing-goods-scaffold-v0`

That branch should add stockpile keys and bucket mappings only. It should not add textile production or consumption by itself.

Full textile production should remain later:

```text
sheep/flax/hemp/cotton -> fiber/wool -> cloth/textiles -> clothing
```

## Suggested Branch Sequence

Recommended order:

1. `docs/soft-need-consumption-model`
2. `feature/clothing-goods-scaffold-v0`
3. `feature/soft-need-consumption-v0`
4. `feature/goods-quality-preference-modifiers-plan`
5. `feature/household-home-inventory-plan`

If household goods consumption happens before clothing goods exist, `feature/soft-need-consumption-v0` should consume only `household_goods` and leave clothing as `unavailable`.

## Open Questions

- Should `feature/soft-need-consumption-v0` consume only household goods first, leaving clothing unavailable until clothing goods are scaffolded?
- Should satisfaction ratios be stored in resources, returned in pressure summaries, or kept in a dedicated demand result structure?
- Should consumption run every simulation tick, monthly, seasonally, or on another interval?
- Should soft-need demand use household count, population, lifecycle stage, or prosperity status per bucket?
- What exact threshold should distinguish `low` from `unmet` for soft needs?
- Should surplus household goods push status to `comfortable`, or should prosperity read only satisfaction ratios after consumption exists?
