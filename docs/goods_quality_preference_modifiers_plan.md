# Goods Quality and Preference Modifiers Plan

This document plans how exact goods should eventually matter after category-based demand is stable. It is a planning document only. It does not implement code, add goods, change consumption, alter prosperity, or introduce money.

## Purpose

Category-based demand is now active:

- goods map to need buckets
- food consumes by bucket as a hard need
- household goods and clothing consume as soft needs
- soft-need resolution records satisfaction ratios

The next design layer is making exact goods meaningful inside those buckets without breaking the readable bucket model. A household should still need `food`, `clothing`, or `household_goods` first. Exact goods should then explain better satisfaction, local flavor, preference, identity, or prosperity potential.

The goal is not to turn every household into an exact-recipe solver. The goal is to make specific goods matter in ways the player can inspect and understand.

## Current Foundation

The current demand model has these foundations:

- `GoodsNeedBucketHelper` maps exact goods to need buckets.
- `City.consume_food()` consumes from the `food` bucket.
- `City.resolve_soft_needs()` consumes household goods and clothing from mapped city stockpiles.
- Need pressure entries report `available`, `remaining`, `consumed_last_period`, `satisfaction_ratio`, `shortage`, and `status`.
- Status vocabulary includes `none`, `unavailable`, `unmet`, `low`, and `met`.
- Household goods demand is generated by housed households.
- Clothing demand is generated from household population.
- Household prosperity is display-only.
- Household inventory does not exist yet.
- UI reads model state and does not consume resources.
- Money, prices, credit, debt, markets, and household savings do not exist.

## Definitions

- `need bucket`: a broad demand category such as `food`, `clothing`, `household_goods`, or `tools`.
- `exact good`: a specific stockpile key that can satisfy one or more buckets, such as `food`, `bread`, `clothing`, `carved_goods`, or `tools`.
- `base satisfaction`: the default amount of need coverage a good provides before modifiers.
- `quality modifier`: mostly global effectiveness of a good inside a bucket.
- `preference modifier`: household, city, culture, or trade-specific desire for a good.
- `variety modifier`: bonus from having multiple useful goods in the same bucket.
- `cultural/local identity modifier`: modifier from a settlement, lineage, region, or tradition valuing a good.
- `prosperity/status demand modifier`: future effect where stable, comfortable, or prosperous households want different goods.
- `substitute good`: a good that can satisfy a bucket imperfectly or temporarily.
- `preferred good`: a good that a household, city, or culture values more than the baseline.
- `luxury good`: a good that should improve comfort, identity, or prosperity rather than basic survival.

## Design Principles

- Categories first, exact goods second.
- Exact goods should improve or flavor satisfaction before they punish.
- Modifiers should be visible, inspectable, or explainable in UI.
- Avoid hidden prosperity penalties.
- Avoid exact household recipes until household inventory exists.
- Keep deterministic ordering and deterministic scoring.
- Keep city-stockpile resolution until household inventory exists.
- Do not smuggle prices, money, credit, debt, or market value into quality modifiers.
- Keep hard needs and soft needs separate.
- Missing soft preferences should usually create comfort/status pressure, not survival failure.

## Quality Vs Preference

Quality is systemic effectiveness within a bucket.

Examples:

- Bread may satisfy `food` better than raw wheat.
- Generic `food` can remain baseline survival supply.
- Finished `clothing` satisfies `clothing`; wool should remain a future textile input unless a later crude-substitution rule explicitly allows it.
- Future metal tools may satisfy `tools` better than crude wooden tools.

Preference is household, city, culture, or trade-specific desire.

Examples:

- A woodworking family may value `carved_goods`.
- A textile family may value clothing variety.
- A river city may prefer fish as a local identity good.
- A stoneworking lineage may value statues or masonry more strongly.

Quality should usually be global. Preference should be local, family-specific, cultural, or trade-specific later.

## Possible Modifier Types

Future modifiers may include:

- satisfaction multiplier
- flat satisfaction bonus
- reserve priority
- comfort bonus
- variety bonus
- spoilage or perishability priority, future only
- local identity bonus
- trade or family familiarity bonus
- luxury or prosperity-tier bonus

Early modifiers should be small and readable. A good default is to calculate a potential satisfaction score before changing consumption behavior.

## Bucket Examples

Food:

- `food`: baseline survival supply.
- `wheat`: raw food; possibly lower or partial satisfaction if edible directly.
- `bread`: prepared food; possible higher satisfaction.
- `fish`: edible supply; may become perishable later.

Household goods:

- `carved_goods`: baseline domestic good.
- `tiles`: home finish good, but may be better reserved for construction or maintenance.
- `statues`: civic/prestige first, household luxury second.
- future `pottery`: strong household/storage good.

Clothing:

- `clothing`: current final need good.
- `wool`: future textile input, not final clothing satisfaction by default.
- future fine clothing: higher quality or luxury clothing.
- future winter clothing: seasonal comfort, later only.

Tools:

- `tools`: baseline current tool good.
- future `wooden_tools`: crude substitute.
- future metal tools: higher-quality tool supply.

## Consumption Priority

Quality and preference can eventually affect consumption order, but that should not happen before the UI can explain why goods were consumed.

Questions to resolve later:

- Should lower-quality goods be consumed first to preserve high-quality goods?
- Should preferred goods be consumed first to maximize satisfaction?
- Should survival satisfaction and comfort satisfaction be allocated separately?
- Should civic goods be protected from household consumption unless surplus exists?

Recommended initial rule:

- deterministic helper order remains the default consumption order
- quality and preference should not alter consumption order at first
- the first implementation should calculate potential satisfaction, not complex allocation
- any later consumption-priority change must include focused tests and a player-readable explanation

## Interaction With Prosperity

Current prosperity is display-only. Soft needs currently do not alter prosperity directly.

Future quality and preference can help determine whether a household is `comfortable` or `prosperous`, but the first step should not make unmet exact preferences severe.

Recommended staged approach:

1. Show base satisfaction and possible quality score without changing prosperity.
2. Show variety or preference notes as display-only.
3. Let soft-need satisfaction affect comfort/prosperity only after the player can inspect why.
4. Keep `struggling` tied to hard needs such as food and shelter, not missing luxury or preference goods.

Missing hard needs can remain serious. Missing soft preferences should be comfort/status pressure only.

## Interaction With Household Inventory

Household inventory should come later. Until then:

- modifiers are city-level or aggregate
- the city resolves stockpile demand
- the model does not track which family owns which good
- households do not receive exact-good recipes
- preferences should be summarized rather than individually allocated

Future household/home inventory may store:

- home food reserves
- clothing
- household goods
- heirlooms
- tools
- emergency buffers

Once household inventory exists, preferences can become more household-specific because the game can explain who has what and why it matters.

## First Future Implementation Recommendation

Safest first code slice:

- add a read-only goods quality metadata helper
- expose quality scores by good and bucket
- add tests proving score lookup is deterministic
- do not change consumption order
- do not change prosperity
- do not add new goods or production chains

Alternative safe slice:

- add a Need Satisfaction Breakdown display
- show which goods are currently satisfying each bucket
- keep consumption behavior unchanged

Recommended first branch after this plan:

- `feature/goods-quality-metadata-v0`

Reason: metadata is lower risk than allocation. It creates a stable vocabulary for quality without quietly changing balance.

## Explicit Non-Goals

- no code changes in this planning branch
- no production chains
- no new goods except docs-only examples
- no prosperity effects
- no household inventory
- no exact household recipes
- no money, prices, credit, debt, or currency
- no market routing
- no hidden penalties
- no spoilage except future-only notes
- no climate or winter effects except future-only notes
- no consumption order changes yet

## Open Questions

- Should quality change amount consumed or satisfaction gained?
- Should preference be household-level, city-level, culture-level, or lineage-level first?
- Should variety require multiple goods in the same bucket?
- Should high-quality goods be preserved or consumed first?
- Should luxury goods affect `comfortable` or `prosperous` status?
- How should UI explain quality without cluttering City Overview?
- Should civic goods such as statues be protected from household use unless explicitly surplus?
- Should local identity bonuses be attached to settlement site, family trade, or city history?
- Should preference be calculated before or after city-stockpile consumption?

## Suggested Branch Sequence

Recommended order:

1. `feature/goods-quality-preference-modifiers-plan`
2. `feature/household-home-inventory-plan`
3. `feature/goods-quality-metadata-v0`
4. `feature/need-satisfaction-breakdown-display-v0`

The inventory plan should happen before exact household preferences become mechanical, because household-specific preference is hard to explain without household-owned goods.
